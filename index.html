This portfolio documents a quarter-long exploration of how seven real companies — from Netflix to Epic Healthcare — deploy AI to solve hard business problems. In each lab, I moved beyond surface-level theory to actually build the systems: recommendation engines, forecasting models, A/B testing frameworks, and agentic AI workflows. What I learned most sharply is that the gap between AI hype and implementation reality lives in the details — data quality, governance design, and the humans-in-the-loop who decide when to trust the output.

💊 EveryCure L01

AI for Drug Repurposing

I built a knowledge graph system that connects FDA-approved drugs to diseases they weren't originally designed to treat, mirroring EveryCure's approach to accelerating rare disease treatment. The core lesson: AI doesn't replace clinical research — it surfaces hypotheses faster so human experts can prioritize the most promising ones.

🎬 Netflix L02

A/B Testing & Experimentation

I designed and analyzed an A/B testing framework modeled on Netflix's artwork personalization engine, evaluating how thumbnail variations affect click-through rates across different viewer segments. The key insight: without a rigorous experimentation culture, even well-intentioned AI improvements can silently degrade the user experience.

I built a collaborative filtering recommendation engine based on Spotify's approach to surfacing music users didn't know they'd love, exploring how user behavior signals translate into personalized discovery at scale. 🚗 Uber L04

Forecasting & Dynamic Pricing

I modeled Uber's surge pricing system, building a demand forecasting pipeline that predicts ride requests by time and location and adjusts pricing in response. The business lesson was stark: a model that's 90% accurate in testing can still produce pricing decisions that feel deeply unfair to customers — and that gap is a governance problem, not a technical one.

Deep Learning & Computer Vision

I explored the computer vision stack behind Waymo's autonomous vehicles — how convolutional neural networks perceive and classify objects in real-time sensor data to make driving decisions. 🏥 Epic Healthcare L07 Capstone

Agentic AI & Insurance Advocacy

I built a fully functional AI agent in n8n — an AI Insurance Advocate that reads denied insurance claims, pulls patient chart evidence, checks policy criteria, and drafts professional appeal letters autonomously. I also designed a governance framework covering scope, data privacy, human oversight, auditing, and monitoring. The deepest lesson: agentic AI is a system design problem, not a prompting problem — the guardrails and oversight model matter as much as the model itself.

Lessons Learned

Key Takeaways

01

AI is only as good as its data pipeline. Across every lab, model performance was bounded not by algorithm sophistication but by how cleanly the underlying data was collected, labeled, and structured. A mediocre model on great data consistently outperforms a great model on messy data.

02

Every AI system needs a human-in-the-loop governance plan. From Waymo's edge-case problem to Epic's insurance appeals, the hardest design question was never "can the AI do this?" — it was "when should a human override it, and who's accountable when it's wrong?"

03

Business value comes from workflow integration, not model accuracy alone. A recommendation engine with 85% accuracy deployed into the right workflow creates more value than a 95%-accurate model that lives in a spreadsheet. The IS professional's job is to close that gap.

04

Agentic AI shifts the design problem from prompting to architecture. Building the Insurance Advocate taught me that Level 3 AI isn't about clever prompts — it's about choosing the right tools, writing the right guardrails, and deciding where humans stay in the loop. That is an information systems job, not just a technical one.
